# **Physics-Informed Machine Learning to Optimize CRISPR Stomatal Engineering for Climate-Resilient Rice in Telangana**

Climate-induced heat stress and severe drought increasingly threaten global food security, particularly for high-water-demand staple crops like paddy ( _Oryza sativa_ ). Plant stomata, which regulate transpirational water loss and CO₂ assimilation, represent critical targets for engineering climate resilience. While CRISPR/Cas9 editing of stomatal regulatory genes—such as the _OsEPF1_ promoter—can effectively alter stomatal density, the R&D bottleneck is severe. Determining the precise biological "sweet spot" that minimizes water loss without severely compromising photosynthetic yield requires multi-season field testing that poses significant temporal and economic burdens. To overcome this limitation, this study presents a purely computational decision-support pipeline utilizing physics-informed machine learning to bypass _in vivo_ trial-and-error. We computationally simulated a comprehensive _OsEPF1_ mutant library representing highly variable degrees of stomatal reduction. An optimized XGBoost regression framework was engineered to ingest circadian environmental data from regional cultivation zones (e.g., Telangana), mapping daily humidity fluctuations and peak heat spikes alongside strict plant thermodynamic parameters, specifically Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD). The algorithm mathematically screens the mutant library against these circadian cycles to forecast the exact transpiration-to-photosynthesis trade-off curve. By identifying the specific percentage of gene expression reduction required to maximize yield under local drought conditions, this predictive oracle provides agricultural geneticists with a highly precise computational target for gRNA design. This framework drastically slashes R&D timelines, converting observational field breeding into computationally guided precision engineering for sustainable agriculture.
